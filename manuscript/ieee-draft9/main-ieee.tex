\begin{abstract}
Short serverless functions are dominated by cold starts and network distance, while capacity close to users sits idle on
consumer machines. Serving latency-sensitive invocations from such volunteer machines is hard for two reasons: owners end
sessions abruptly, which destroys every warm sandbox on the host, and owners are untrusted and can read or tamper with
tenant code and data. Volunteer-computing systems cope through redundant execution and long deadlines, and serverless
schedulers assume trusted, always-on fleets. We observe that remote attestation of a locked, measured appliance image
reduces a malicious owner who controls software, power and network to omission and timing failures. What remains is a scheduling problem over \emph{attested
session state}: tenant keys provisioned on a host and warm sandboxes are expensive to establish, are lost when the session
ends, and every additional key holder enlarges the tenant's attack surface. We present \voss{}, a scheduler built around
this problem. A slow control loop plans where keys and warm state live, with coverage that stays feasible when any $f$
hosts defect; a fast loop places each invocation with a cost model and hedges risky ones. Failure detection uses
end-to-end progress instead of liveness, which exposes gray failures; scheduler failover uses leases and epoch
fencing without consensus among volunteers; and attested receipts make accounting verifiable. In a deterministic simulator
calibrated on a TPM-equipped machine, \voss{} meets a 3\,s sync P99 target in \ViabAPThreeVoss{} of \ViabACells{} cells (churn by adversary fraction) and
\ViabBPThreeVoss{} of \ViabBCells{} (substrate by load), where the strongest simple baseline never does.
Planning lowers P99 by \GreedyNoneMed--\GreedyNoneHigh\% against no planning, and progress-based detection keeps success
at \VossSuccAlpha\% with 20\% omitting owners where heartbeat-only detection falls to \HbSuccAlpha\%. Several designed
components do not pay off in our experiments (geo-clustering, reputation, and tiered caching), an exact solver is not
distinguishable from a greedy heuristic at our sample size, and a commercial-style reference remains faster; we report these with all other hypotheses.
\end{abstract}
