\paragraph{Keep-alive and autoscaling.} Serverless platforms decide how long to keep a sandbox after an invocation
and when to start one ahead of demand. Shahrad et al.\ characterise a production workload and set both times from a
per-application histogram of idle times~\cite{shahrad2020wild}. FaaSCache treats warm sandboxes as a cache with
greedy-dual eviction~\cite{fuerst2021faascache}. Autoscalers such as Knative's size the number of sandboxes from
observed concurrency~\cite{knativekpa}. Work on faster starts restores sandboxes from snapshots~\cite{ao2022faasnap,
cadden2020seuss} or shares runtime state~\cite{oakes2018sock}. All assume that the machine holding a sandbox stays,
that any machine may hold any function, and that machines are interchangeable. We use these policies as baselines and
snapshots as a mechanism.

\paragraph{Machines of different speeds.} Paragon and Quasar classify applications by how they perform on each
machine type and under interference, and assign them accordingly~\cite{delimitrou2013paragon,delimitrou2014quasar};
Gavel makes scheduling policies for accelerators aware of per-job throughput on each
type~\cite{narayanan2020gavel}. These systems schedule long-running services and jobs on machines that stay. Our
run-time model has the same aim for functions that run for milliseconds, adds a per-worker term because volunteer
machines of one model differ, and feeds a choice that also weighs whether each machine will still be there.

\paragraph{Capacity that can be taken away.} Zhang et al.\ run serverless functions on harvested virtual machines
that the provider can evict, with a placement policy aware of eviction~\cite{zhang2021harvest}. The machines belong to
the provider, are trusted, and announce evictions. Volunteer machines belong to others, leave without notice, and
must hold a key before they can serve.

\paragraph{Volunteer computing.} BOINC and SETI@home run batch computations on volunteered
machines~\cite{anderson2004boinc,anderson2002seti} and establish correctness by running each task on several machines
and comparing. Availability of such machines has been measured and modelled in the Failure Trace
Archive~\cite{javadi2013fta,javadi2009seti}, which we use as input. Deadlines there are hours to days. We replace
replication of every task by attestation, and re-execute only a sample.

\paragraph{Confidential serverless.} Confidential virtual machines and enclaves protect a function from the
operator of the machine~\cite{costan2016sgx,amd2020snp,arnautov2016scone}; recent work reduces their start-up cost for
serverless workloads~\cite{holmes2024severifast,segarra2024sesame,wallet2025} and measures their
overheads~\cite{misono2025cvm,li2023bifrost}. These systems target server processors in datacenters. Consumer
machines mostly lack such hardware, and have a TPM instead; we build on measured boot of the whole machine, which is
why a session excludes the owner's own use.

\paragraph{Attestation on consumer machines.} Keylime attests machines with TPMs at boot and
continuously~\cite{schear2016keylime}, following the roles of the remote attestation
architecture~\cite{rfc9334}. Firmware TPMs have been broken with physical access~\cite{jacob2023faultpm}, which is
the reason for our class~P and the sampled audit.

\paragraph{Detecting failures from request outcomes.} Failure detectors based on heartbeats, including accrual
detectors~\cite{hayashibara2004phi}, observe whether a process is reachable. Studies of production incidents show
that components often stay reachable while they stop doing their work~\cite{huang2017gray}. Proxies such as Envoy
remove a server after consecutive failed requests~\cite{envoyoutlier}, and sending a second request after a
latency percentile reduces tail latency when servers are slow~\cite{dean2013tail}. Our failure handling differs in
three ways: the timer comes from a per-attempt prediction, not from a fixed value or a latency history; the second
attempt is restricted to key holders in another failure domain; and the outcome of every attempt updates a per-worker
estimate that provisioning uses.
