\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\todoitem{T25}{held-out experiments}{When the results hold, why, and what would change them: written after the held-out experiments.}

\paragraph{The owner cannot use a lent machine.} Attestation covers the whole machine from boot, so a session
excludes the owner's own use. The design applies to machines that are idle for whole periods, such as office machines
at night, and not to machines with spare cores during use.

\paragraph{Availability data.} The availability trace is from 2007--2009 and records when a host's processor was free
for a volunteer project. Machines lent by booting an image may follow other patterns; in particular a restart into
the owner's system ends a session, where the trace would show a short gap. The archived copy of the trace is
truncated.

\paragraph{Functions that may run twice.} Backup attempts and the audit apply only to functions that the tenant
declares safe to run twice for one request. For other functions a dropped result becomes a missed deadline, and on
class-P machines a forged result is not detected.

\paragraph{Physical attacks.} For class-P owners the design gives no confidentiality. A tenant with confidential
code or data restricts its functions to classes S and M, which shrinks the pool to the machines whose owners are not
expected to attack them or whose memory is encrypted. We do not measure how many volunteer machines fall in each
class.

\paragraph{The provider.} The provider's verifier decides which machines are attested, and its routers and scheduler
can refuse service. The design removes the provider's ability to read functions and their data, and does not remove
its ability to deny them.

\paragraph{One scheduler.} All decisions are made by one scheduler, whose measured capacity
(Section~\ref{sec:res-scale}) bounds the pool at a given load. The bound is for one scheduler.

\paragraph{Guarantees hold for the model.} Propositions~\ref{prop:submodular} and~\ref{prop:shared} bound the greedy
choice against the best choice under the model objective. The Erlang loss formula assumes Poisson arrivals and
treats an invocation that finds no free core as lost, while the system queues it; the deadline probability treats the
wait, start-up and transfer terms at their predicted values.

\paragraph{What is simulated.} The network, the sessions and the scale are simulated. We measure worker costs on
\VMachCount{} machines of \VMachProcClasses{} processor classes (Section~\ref{sec:method}). Machines unlike these
are represented by the nearest measured one.
