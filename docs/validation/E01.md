# E01: simulator against a single-box emulation (P9b)

**Question.** Does the simulator's end-to-end behaviour match a run of the same scenario with real Firecracker microVMs
on real hardware? Design and deviations: D-72 (`docs/DECISIONS.md`); code in `tools/emu/`; scenarios in `configs/e01/`;
data in `runs-e01/` (the emulation runs cannot be regenerated bit for bit, so they are committed); report tables and CDFs
in `runs-e01/report/`.

## Setup

- **Machine.** The lab node (Intel i7-12700, 125 GB, NVMe), reserved for the work, Ubuntu 22.04, kernel 5.15, Firecracker
  v1.10.1 (checksum-verified), CPU governor `powersave` (not changed), emulator and VMs pinned to the 8 P-cores.
- **Bundle.** `configs/calibration/measured/c0-9132c8a291`, measured on this node with the non-reboot suites only. The
  session boot (`reboot_to_kernel`, `appliance_userspace`) keeps its placeholder on both sides.
- **Emulated.** Start-up (boot, snapshot restore, snapshot create), dispatch and function execution run in real
  microVMs. Network, key release, attestation, queueing and the TPM stay models on a wall-clock kernel. Policy code runs
  for real (decision clock `measured`, scale 0); the simulator uses `modeled`.
- **Scale.** 16 donor slices (hardware class `E0`, 2 slots each), Europe only, five functions (dynamic-html, uploader,
  thumbnailer, compression, graph-pagerank; video-processing is excluded, see below), 10 minutes per run, seeds 1-3,
  one run at a time.
- **Scenarios.** `s1_stable` (3 inv/s), `s2_churn` (3 inv/s, sessions of minutes), `s4_load` (100 inv/s),
  `s3_omit_load` (100 inv/s, 20% omitting owners). `s3_omit` (3 inv/s) is kept for the record: at that load one warm
  worker serves everything and the omitting owners never received work, so it says nothing about detection.
- **Metrics and cut-offs** were fixed in `tools/emu/compare.py` before the runs: relative error of sync P50 and P99,
  KS distance, success and deadline-met difference, start-level total variation; verdict `close` / `partly` / `far`.

## Result

| scenario | seeds | P50 err | P99 err | KS | success (pp) | met (pp) | level TV | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| s1_stable | 3 | -6% | +314% | 0.26 | +0.0 | -0.0 | 0.00 | partly |
| s2_churn | 3 | -13% | +270% | 0.34 | +0.0 | +0.1 | 0.00 | partly |
| s3_omit (1 seed) | 1 | -23% | -11% | 0.51 | +0.0 | +0.2 | 0.00 | partly |
| s3_omit_load | 3 | +6% | -6% | 0.20 | +0.2 | -2.6 | 0.00 | partly |
| s4_load | 3 | -10% | -3% | 0.29 | +0.6 | +0.5 | 0.00 | partly |

(error = (simulator - emulation) / emulation, mean over seeds.)

**Reading.**

- **Agrees:** success rate (within 0.6 points everywhere), the mix of start levels (L0-L3: total variation 0.00), the
  median latency (within 6-23%), and the order of magnitude of every latency component. The deadline-met difference is
  largest under omitting owners (-2.6 points).
- **Does not meet our cut-offs:** the *shape* of the latency distribution (KS 0.20-0.51, so every scenario is
  `partly`, none `close`), and the tail at light load. The light-load P99 error (+270% to +314% on seed means) is driven
  by a handful of cold invocations (about 530 sync requests per run, so P99 is the five slowest); per seed the tails are
  close in some seeds (seed 1 of `s1_stable`: 0.160 s simulated against 0.178 s emulated) and far in others. At
  100 inv/s the emulated tail was heavier in one seed (P99 2.6 s against 0.17 s in seed 1 of `s4_load`) and the seed
  means agree (-3%), so three seeds do not separate a real tail effect from seed noise.
- **Component level:** absolute differences are milliseconds. In the simulator wait, start-up and execution medians are
  near zero for warm starts; in the emulation execution is real (about 4 ms median) and the modelled decision step is 2-29%
  shorter than the real one, so relative component errors are large and uninformative.
- **What was not checked:** detection and failover under real timing at scale (the omission scenario has 3 seeds and
  16 workers), the hard-disk tier (switched off in both), the session boot, and any network effect.

## Limits and bookkeeping

- Single node, 16 workers, 3 seeds; hardware class `E0` is a slice of the node, not a consumer PC. The cut-offs are
  judgements, not statistics.
- Emulation counters over the 13 runs: 3,176 VMs killed by reconciliation, 22 restores for which the model expected a
  snapshot that did not exist (booted instead), 5 failed snapshot restores (booted instead), 12 claims without a VM
  (booted); the worst delay of the wall-clock kernel was 67 ms.
- Boot is retried once if the VMM dies while being configured (added after the first batch; it affects
  `s3_omit_load-emu-1` only).
- `video-processing` is excluded: the guest must build its 5 MB input (3.5 s of ffmpeg), which a real invocation receives
  over the network, so its measured time would be input generation.
- The numeric canary reports a mismatch on this node (glibc 2.35, Alder Lake), yet the simulator reproduced the committed
  E09 seed-1 hashes (5 of 5) here: the canary is stricter than the results need on this platform.
