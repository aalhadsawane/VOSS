# Pilot results

Pilots are small runs that exercise each experiment end to end before the paper runs (P10). Every table here is
regenerated from run logs by the scripts named below; numbers come from the **placeholder calibration bundle**
(literature/assumed values) until P9 lands measured data.

| Pilot | Regenerate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [E03](E03.md) predictors × scalarizers | `python -m experiments.runner configs/experiments/E03.yaml --jobs 3` then `python -m experiments.analysis.e03 runs/E03` | 150 workers, 10 min, 8 inv/s, 5 seeds. Predictors are indistinguishable here because the simulated truth equals the profile prior and workers are only speed-scaled; BLR's worker offsets matter once throttling adversaries (P6) and measured SeBS profiles (P9) differ from the prior. Conformal calibration keeps 90% intervals at 0.88-0.89 coverage. Scalarizers differ by < CI width in benign regimes. |
| [E02](E02.md) planning pool × T_plan | `… E02.yaml`, `… p4 E02 runs/E02` | 300 workers, MILP, **measured** decision clock (solver wall time enters simulated time; run alone). Sync P99 is best with a 40-worker pool replanned every 15 s (1.37 s); 10-20 workers cost ~1-2 s per solve for +5-8% P99. At 80 workers a solve takes 17-24 s, longer than `T_plan` = 15 s, and exposure jumps to 33: plans are stale on arrival. This supports the cluster target of ~30 workers (D-19) that P5 uses. |
| [E04](E04.md) planners | `python -m experiments.runner configs/experiments/E04.yaml --jobs 3` then `python -m experiments.analysis.p4 E04 runs/E04` | 200 workers, 15 min, 10 inv/s, two churn regimes, 7 paired systems × 5 seeds. MILP has the lowest sync P99 (1.48 s vs 1.75 s without planning, churn_med) and greedy sits between (1.57 s); keep-alive TTL alone gets 1.66 s at the lowest exposure (12.2). The MILP pays for it with ~30% more idle warm memory than greedy (100 GB vs 77 GB cluster-wide), the highest exposure (19.4 vs 17.2) and ~5 s per solve. Dropping robustness cuts exposure to 14.2 for +4% P99; dropping the exposure term changes nothing at weight 1 (E23 shows why). P2P volumes predate D-37 (slow peers were still used). |
| [E09](E09.md) cache policy | `… E09.yaml`, `… p4 E09 runs/E09` | 200 workers, 15 min, churn_high, tight SSD, greedy planner. GDSF vs LRU, P2P and the HDD tier are within CI of each other here: 15 min is too short for eviction pressure to build. Needs longer horizons and version churn (P8). |
| [E23](E23.md) exposure × robustness | `… E23.yaml`, `… p4 E23 runs/E23` | MILP, exposure weight × f. The robustness level is the main lever: exposure rises 13.6 → 20 → 24 for f = 0 → 0.1 → 0.2 while sync P99 falls ~6-10%, and solve time grows to ~12 s at f = 0.2. The exposure weight matters only at f = 0 (15.1 → 13.6); robust coverage constraints dominate otherwise. |
| [E05](E05.md) geo vs global | `python -m experiments.runner configs/experiments/E05.yaml --jobs 3` then `python -m experiments.analysis.p5 E05 runs/E05` | `configs/pilot.yaml`: 600 workers (~225 attested), 15 min, 20 inv/s; clustering mode × dispersion (EU, EU+NA, world). **H7 is not supported at this scale:** a single global scheduler (`none_global`) has the lowest sync P99 except for `static_regions` on EU+NA (−3%); `dynamic` trails it by +18% (EU), +9% (EU+NA), +7% (world, CI includes 0). The global scheduler picks the fastest of all workers (exec p99 0.88-0.90 s vs ~0.97 s), and decision latency at ~225 workers is still small. Clusters also hold 3.5-4.5× the key exposure: each plans an f-robust trust set for its region. Geo's advantages (decision latency, locality) need larger N and load: see E18 (P8). |
| [E07](E07.md) routing, flash crowd | `… E07.yaml`, `… p5 E07 runs/E07` | Same base; 5× the top function from London for t ∈ [300, 600) s. `score` (with D-33 measured prices) is within +8-10% of `nearest` on overall sync P99 and beats it in the flash window (1.46 s vs 1.81 s), but `po2_nearby` and `warm_aware` handle the flash best (~1.27-1.29 s): trust-cold avoidance still diverts some traffic farther than needed. A first version with fixed penalties lost to `nearest` by 18-30% (D-33). |
| [E06](E06.md) dynamic vs static clusters | `… E06.yaml`, `… p5 E06 runs/E06` | 30 min, two churn regimes. `dynamic` beats `static_kmeans` by 3.8% sync P99 under high churn and 1.5% under low churn (paired CIs exclude 0) with 0.02-0.06 moves per worker-hour and **at most 1 move per worker in any hour** (bound 3, D-34): the P5 exit criterion on bounded migrations. Skew and diurnal factors are left to P8. |
| [E10](E10.md) homogeneous benign null | `… E10.yaml`, `… p5 E10 runs/E10` | Dedicated donors, low churn, no adversaries; homogeneous (C1 only) vs mixed hardware. `simple` = static regions + nearest + least-loaded + keep-alive TTL + no hedging. VOSS still cuts sync P99 by 42-51% (cold starts and slow workers dominate `simple`'s tail), but **simple wins on exposure** (about half of VOSS's key-holder exposure) and slightly on network time. |
| [E15](E15.md) scheduler faults | `… E15.yaml`, `python -m experiments.analysis.p6 E15 runs/E15` | `pilot.yaml`; each fault kind on three clusters' schedulers for 60 s. **Progress detection vs heartbeat-only (H-FT1/H-FT2):** black hole detected in ~6 s vs never (fault-window sync P99 1.15 s vs 2.86 s, 0 vs 116 timeouts); crash 2.3 s vs 5 s; partition 2.1 s vs 5 s; a 400× slower scheduler hands over after ~24 s (decision-latency SLO) vs never. Failover (successor active) adds < 0.5 s; warm standbys are promoted. No invocation lost in any run. |
| [E08](E08.md) adversaries | `… E08.yaml`, `… p6 E08 runs/E08` | α ∈ {0.1, 0.2}, mixed and all-behavior regimes. Progress detection keeps success at 99.9-100% vs 96-99% heartbeat-only, with 5-14× fewer omitted results (omission evidence steers placement away within one or two failures) and suspicion precision 0.95-1.0. Heartbeat-only's sync P99 can look lower only because its failures become timeouts, which leave the successful-invocation P99. Dropping robustness (`f0`) raises P99 even in benign runs (fewer trust-warm workers). Waste: 2-4% extra attempts per success (6% heartbeat-only). |
| [E16](E16.md) hedging | `… E16.yaml`, `… p6 E16 runs/E16` | Risk-proportional hedging θ × budget vs off/always, benign and α = 0.1. **H-FT3 supported:** with omitting adversaries, θ = 0.3 / budget 5% cuts sync P99 from 1.57 s (off) to 1.23 s at 1.6% extra attempts, while `always` pays 10.6% for 1.49 s; benign, θ = 0.1 / 20% gives −11% P99 at 2.7%. Late duplicate results (hedge losers) stay at 3-9 per 1000 vs 39-41 for `always`. |
| [E14](E14.md) donation sessions | `… E14.yaml`, `… p6 E14 runs/E14` | 30 min, four churn regimes × owners sending fake drain notices (α = 0.2). Viable everywhere: 100% success, sync P99 1.14-1.19 s across regimes without fake drains. Fake drains cost 2-9% P99 and slightly more waste: they only make the scheduler more conservative (doc 07 §4), never excuse an omission. No invocation lost. |
| [E-CS1 (simulated)](ECS1-sim.md) | `python -m experiments.ecs1 --n 200` | Lifecycle engine per substrate on an isolated C0 host. The appliance pays a reboot per donation session (~19 s), CVM classes pay 1-8 s per wCVM; warm (L3) starts are ms everywhere. Measured side arrives with P9. |

**Caveat (P3/P4 pilots).** E03, E04, E09, E23 and E02 build on `configs/smoke.yaml`, which pins the sync placer to
`least_loaded` (a P2 setting). Scalarizers therefore only affect async/filler placement and admission in E03, and
planners act through warm/trust state only. P5+ pilots use `configs/pilot.yaml` with VOSS's default placers; the
full experiments (P8) rerun these on it.
